Outcomes trend

Median 150% graduation rate across four-year institutions rose from 47.1% in 2015 to 52.4% in 2023. Median first-year retention barely moved, holding at roughly 74–75% the whole period — graduation improved without retention leading it.

More time lifts both groups — Pell +11.9 pp and non-Pell +13.6 pp between years 4 and 8 — so the gap does not close with time: 14.6 pp at four years, 16.6 pp at six, 16.3 pp at eight (horizon gaps from the rounded rates).

03

What predicts graduation

Mean absolute SHAP values from the Outcomes Lab model — the average impact each feature has on a predicted 150% graduation rate, in percentage points. One feature towers over everything else: first-year retention, at 9.49 pp. Who an institution enrolls and how selective it is matter far less than whether its first-years come back.

Model trained on 2015–2019, tested on 2021–2023 (2020 excluded), 5,389 test institutions, cohorts of 50 or more. Test performance: R² 0.745, MAE 7.15 pp, RMSE 10.01 pp.

By sector

Cohort-weighted 150% graduation rates pooled across 2015–2023, four-year institutions. Private nonprofits lead; for-profits trail by more than 30 points — yet their internal Pell gaps are the smallest, because completion is low for everyone there.

How to read this — methodology

150% graduation rate
The share of a first-time, full-time entering cohort that graduates within 150% of normal time (six years for a bachelor’s degree).
Award rate
From IPEDS Outcome Measures: the share of an entering cohort earning any credential within 4, 6, or 8 years. Cohort rates here are student-weighted, so large institutions count more.
Medians
Unless a figure says “cohort-weighted,” medians are institution medians across four-year institutions — the typical college, not the typical student.
SHAP values
The model’s average impact per feature, in percentage points: how much knowing that feature typically moves a predicted graduation rate.
The model
Trained 2015–2019, tested 2021–2023 with 2020 excluded; 5,389 test institutions; cohorts of 50+ only. R² 0.745, MAE 7.15 pp against a 16.3 pp naive baseline.
