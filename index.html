TL;DR — THE 20-SECOND VERSION Tencent's new flagship learned to see screens and click through apps, Moonshot dropped the biggest open model download anyone has ever posted, and Claude helped scientists solve a protein puzzle that has sat unsolved for years — plus OpenAI and Meta both bought coding-agent tools on the very same day, a $32 million model that out-codes giants, and a security AI that attacks your own systems before real hackers can.
01TENCENTCOMPUTER USENOV 05, 2026

Tencent's Hy4 Can Finally See — and It Can Use Your Computer Too

Tencent released the full version of Hy4 on November 4, and for the first time in the Hunyuan line it can read images, charts and documents — and then actually operate software, through both the command line and the on-screen interface. It is a huge model, 770 billion parameters with about 49 billion active at a time and a 1 million token memory, trained even on pro tools like Blender and CAD. You can try it today through Tencent's apps and OpenRouter; the downloadable weights are promised in the coming days, and Tencent is honest that it still trails Claude and GPT on the hardest tests.

WHY IT MATTERSAn AI that can see your screen and do the clicking itself is a different kind of tool than a chatbot. This is the computer-use race going fully global — and going open.
SOURCE →
02MOONSHOT AIOPEN WEIGHTSNOV 05, 2026

The Biggest Open AI Download Ever: 2.8 Trillion Parameters, Free to Take

Moonshot AI has posted the weights for Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face — at 2.8 trillion total parameters across about 1.56 terabytes of files, it is the largest open-weight model anyone has ever made downloadable. Only about 104 billion parameters switch on for each answer, which keeps it runnable, and Moonshot says it rivals the top closed US models. The release landed November 2 and spread everywhere by November 4.

WHY IT MATTERSThe open-versus-closed gap keeps shrinking in the most literal way possible — file size. Anyone with serious hardware can now hold a frontier-scale model in their own building.
SOURCE →
03ANTHROPICSCIENCENOV 05, 2026

Claude Helped Solve a Protein Puzzle Hidden Inside a Virus

Anthropic published new biology work on November 4: using Claude throughout the process, its team resolved the first structure of a vault — a huge barrel-shaped protein particle — found in a bacteriophage, a virus that infects bacteria. Claude helped screen some 15 billion predicted proteins, design the experiments, and process the microscope data; a purification step that used to take days took about 90 minutes, and the structure of 96 protein copies was assembled in 34 hours.

WHY IT MATTERSThis is AI doing real lab science alongside humans, not just chatting about it — and a simpler version of a mysterious particle gives biologists a new way to study how these structures form.
SOURCE →
04OPENAICODING AGENTSNOV 05, 2026

OpenAI Is Buying the Toolbox Under Its Coding Agent

OpenAI said on November 4 that it will acquire Pierre Computer, the small startup behind Code Storage and open-source developer tools like Trees, Diffs and Highlights — tools Codex already uses. Pierre built its storage on Git but designed it for machines: lots of AI agents reading and writing code at the same time, which is exactly where coding is heading. Pierre's open-source projects will stay open source after the deal.

WHY IT MATTERSWhoever owns the place where agent-written code lives owns a big piece of the future workflow. OpenAI is quietly building that layer before anyone else locks it up.

05METACODING AGENTSNOV 05, 2026

Same Day, Same Idea: Meta Bought a Conductor for Coding Agents

Also on November 4, Meta acquired Conductor, a startup founded in 2024 whose whole job is running several AI coding agents at once, each in its own workspace — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and others side by side. Conductor has stopped new sign-ups, its cloud workspaces go read-only on November 13, and its team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs to build new developer interfaces.

WHY IT MATTERSWhen two giants buy agent-orchestration tools on the same day, the message is clear: the next coding contest is not one smart agent, it is who manages a whole team of them best.
SOURCE →
06RICURSIVE INTELLIGENCEOPEN WEIGHTSNOV 05, 2026

Two Ex-Googlers Built America's Best Open Coding Model for $32 Million

Ricursive Intelligence — founded by Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini, the researchers behind Google's AlphaChip — released Ripple-65B Preview, a free Apache 2.0 model that tops US open models on two tough coding benchmarks, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench Pro. It has 65 billion parameters, a 1 million token context, and is live on Hugging Face, Together AI and OpenRouter. The striking number is the cost: about $32 million to build, a fraction of what the giants spend. These are the company's own benchmark claims, not yet independently checked.

WHY IT MATTERSIf a small, focused team can reach the top of the open coding charts for $32 million, raw spending stops being the moat — good ideas get a vote again.
SOURCE →
07CROWDSTRIKESECURITYNOV 05, 2026

Now the Security AI Attacks You First — on Purpose

CrowdStrike unveiled Agentic Adversary Emulation at Fal.Con Europe on November 4. Inside its Falcon platform, an offensive AI called Red Tempest chains together your small weaknesses — a misconfiguration here, a loose permission there — into a real attack path toward your critical systems, tells you what to fix first, then attacks again to prove the path is closed. CrowdStrike's own research documented an AI agent chaining one flaw into access to 726 databases with no human at the keyboard, which is exactly why it built this. It is in early access for select customers.

WHY IT MATTERSAttackers are already using agents that never sleep. A defender that rehearses the attack continuously, instead of once a year, is what keeping up actually looks like.
