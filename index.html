AI Radar · Daily Brief

What AI did today

AI is moving stupid fast, and nobody can keep up by scrolling all day — so I keep a radar. Here’s the short version: what’s new, what to watch out for, and what genuinely blew my mind. Every source competes on merit — the big labs, the startups, the open-source world, all of it.

ai-radar — daily brief / TUE · OCT 20, 2026 · EDITION 003 FEED SYNCED LATEST BRIEF
TL;DR — THE 20-SECOND VERSION Today: a model that writes in parallel instead of word by word, agents that live on your own machines, a mystery model unmasked — and the money race behind it all. No hype, just the stuff worth your five minutes.
01INCEPTIONDIFFUSION MODELSOCT 20, 2026

A model that writes in parallel, not one word at a time

Inception released Mercury 3, a diffusion language model that generates and refines many tokens at once instead of one by one. It runs at over 1,000 tokens per second, has a 1 million token context window, and the company says it beats GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 on quality benchmarks.

WHY IT MATTERSIf diffusion models keep scaling, the speed limit we are used to with chatbots could change completely.
SOURCE →
02NVIDIAOPEN MODELSOCT 19, 2026

NVIDIA wants agents that live on your own machines

NVIDIA launched Nemotron 3.5, a family of open-weight models built for always-on agents. The big one, Super VL at 120B parameters, is for enterprise work like document review and research, and smaller Bolt models are made to run locally on AI PCs. Companies like EY, Salesforce, CrowdStrike, and Perplexity are already listed as users.

WHY IT MATTERSThis is a real option for teams that want capable agents without sending everything to a cloud API.
SOURCE →
03MINIMAXOPENROUTEROCT 20, 2026

The mystery “Space Bunny” model finally has a name

That stealth model that topped OpenRouter for 10 straight days? It was MiniMax M3.1 Flash. MiniMax says it delivers frontier-level agentic performance at much lower cost, with median output above 170 tokens per second, and the weights are promised on October 23. It is free for 72 hours on OpenRouter starting October 19.

WHY IT MATTERSGood reminder to judge models by real usage, not just launch day — this one proved itself before anyone knew its name.
SOURCE →
04CLOUDFLAREEXTRACTIONOCT 20, 2026

An AI that extracts instead of generates

Cloudflare previewed Clairic, a new kind of model that pulls numbers, dates, and names straight out of text and images in one pass, usually in under a second. No word-by-word generation, and billing is input-only at $0.10 per million tokens. Cloudflare says it cuts processing time by about 70% versus using a regular LLM for extraction.

WHY IT MATTERSFor invoices, forms, and messy everyday documents, a fast extractor can be more useful than a bigger chatbot.
SOURCE →
05OPENAICHATGPT SLIDESOCT 20, 2026

ChatGPT Slides just became a team tool

OpenAI added real-time collaboration to ChatGPT Slides — you can share a deck, edit together, leave comments, and click right on a slide to fix text, charts, or images. You present from ChatGPT or export to PowerPoint or Google Slides. It is rolling out to Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans.

WHY IT MATTERSThe deck no longer has to leave the conversation where it started, which saves a lot of back-and-forth for teams.
SOURCE →
06OPENAILEGACY CODEOCT 20, 2026

ChatGPT is learning the old code nobody wants to touch

OpenAI says its latest models are getting better at low-resource and legacy languages like COBOL, Fortran, and Visual Basic, with new evaluations including SQLite and COBOL migration. These are the languages that still run a lot of banks, governments, and large companies.

WHY IT MATTERSReal modernization work lives in old systems — help there is practical, not flashy, and it matters.
SOURCE →
07OPENAIFUNDINGOCT 20, 2026

The money race behind the model race

Reuters reports OpenAI is moving fast to close a roughly $30 billion round that could value it above $1.4 trillion, ahead of Anthropic’s expected IPO in mid-November. Backers in talks include SoftBank and Abu Dhabi-based MGX. Neither company has confirmed the details.

WHY IT MATTERSHow these two raise and spend money will shape pricing, access, and how long the current pace can last.
