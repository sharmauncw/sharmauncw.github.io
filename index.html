01AWSAI INFRASTRUCTUREOCT 24, 2026

The cloud is sold out — through 2027

Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said on the a16z Show, released October 23, that AWS capacity for 2026 is sold out, 2027 is largely sold out, and a good chunk of 2028 is already spoken for. AWS still holds back some capacity for on-demand use, but the big long-term chunks are gone — even with about $220 billion in planned 2026 spending, and an AI business that has passed a $25 billion annual run rate.

WHY IT MATTERSThe bottleneck in AI is no longer just smarter models. It is power, chips and buildings — and the biggest cloud on earth cannot build them fast enough.

02GOOGLE CLOUDMODEL GARDENOCT 25, 2026

Google Cloud now sells its rivals' best models

Google Cloud's Vertex AI Model Garden has opened a private preview of OpenAI's GPT-6 models — Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Luna — and made Anthropic-managed Claude deployments generally available, including Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1. In the same breath, Sundar Pichai warned that compute shortages may last into 2027.

WHY IT MATTERSClouds are competing on choice now. Even Google would rather host the best model for the job — whoever built it — than lose the customer.

03HUGGING FACEOPEN SCIENCEOCT 22, 2026

You can now chat with DNA — for free

Hugging Face released Carbon-X, a free open model (Apache 2.0) that takes raw DNA in an ordinary chat and answers in English — is this mutation harmful, what does this region do, where does this sequence come from? At 14.8 billion parameters it averages 66.7% across five DNA benchmarks, close to GPT-6.1 Sol's 67.5%, at roughly 1/1,800 of the cost per problem. It fits on a single 80GB GPU, so labs can keep sensitive sequences on their own machines.

WHY IT MATTERSNear-frontier genetics help that a lab can run privately, for free — Hugging Face estimates analysing all 2.4 million uncertain variants in ClinVar would cost about $300 with Carbon-X, versus $32,000 to $4 million with frontier models.
SOURCE →
04OPEN SOURCEPHONE AGENTSOCT 25, 2026

Phone agents caught up — and variety was the trick

Researchers from Nanjing University, SenseTime and partner universities released OpenMobile-2: two open smartphone-agent models (9B and 27B, built on Qwen) plus the full training environment, dataset and setup to rebuild them from scratch. Their agents practised inside a simulated world of more than 110 apps — and the surprise finding was that variety beat volume: the same amount of training data, spread across more apps, lifted success on apps the model had never seen from 60.7% to 67.5%.

WHY IT MATTERSIf you want an agent that handles the app it has never seen, train it on more kinds of tasks, not just more of the same — and now anyone can test that recipe themselves.
SOURCE →
05BLOCKVOICE AIOCT 23, 2026

A voice model that listens while it talks

Block unveiled Dessa, a speech model built for full-duplex conversation — it can listen and speak at the same time, the way people actually talk, instead of taking turns like a walkie-talkie. Block says it learned from recorded human speech alone, with no text and no pretrained models, carries pauses and emotion, and runs locally on a MacBook Pro M4 Max or better. Fair warning: there is no public access yet, and Block has shared recordings rather than benchmarks, so the claims are still untested.

WHY IT MATTERSVoice AI stops feeling robotic the moment it can be interrupted naturally — and running on your own machine keeps the conversation off the cloud.

06ANTHROPICFUTURE OF WORKOCT 16, 2026

Inside Anthropic, agents log six hours for every human hour

Anthropic published a rare look inside its own workplace: by August, the Claude agents its employees direct were logging six times as many active work hours as the people directing them. Engineers are shipping about five times as many code commits, staff spend a growing share of their time — about 22% by spring — on the meta-work of briefing and reviewing agents, and the junior share of new technical hires has fallen from 27% to 9%.

WHY IT MATTERSThis is the clearest inside evidence yet of what agent-first work does to the entry-level ladder — the job shifts from doing the first draft to supervising a small army that does.
